Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Atlanta

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

The age of your equipment plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the first heat wave of summer.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.

Common HVAC Problems in Atlanta

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of problems. Long cooling seasons push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and salt air and moisture chew at outdoor condenser coils and fins, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Clogged filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, a slow kind of damage most homeowners never see coming.

We also see the full range of component failures in local homes: weak capacitors that leave the condenser humming without starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are far cheaper to address early. If your system is doing anything unusual, new noises, weak airflow, rooms that won't hold temperature, bills creeping upward, a quick diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes a costly breakdown.
