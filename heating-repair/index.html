The Benefits of Professional Heating Repair

Heating repair carries the highest stakes in home comfort work because combustion is involved. A furnace that lights late booms gas vapor inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal; a heat exchanger that cracks can pass combustion gases into the supply air; a flue that has stopped drafting sends carbon monoxide into the house instead of out of it, and none of these announce themselves in ways an untrained eye can catch. The diagnostic terrain is genuinely tricky too: a furnace that short-cycles might have a failing limit switch, or it might be protecting itself from a clogged filter, and cleaning a flame sensor without asking why it fouled leaves the next lockout already scheduled.

That is why Marietta HVAC does not work on hunches. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate so the blower and the fire are properly matched. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve before blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running in the background and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because that is what the machine heating your home deserves.

Heating Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Replace filters monthly through the heating season to keep airflow strong and the heat exchanger from overheating
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
  • Never store paints, solvents, or chlorine products near the furnace; their vapors corrode the burners
  • Book a fall tune-up so worn parts are caught before the first cold night
  • Take booming ignition or new rattles seriously; both are cheap to fix early and expensive late
  • Leave open space around the furnace so the burners can draw enough combustion air
  • Install a CO detector on each floor and test them every month
